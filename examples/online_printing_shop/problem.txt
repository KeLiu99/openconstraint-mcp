Online printing shop scheduling (OPS) — sequence-flexible FJSP core.

Problem
-------

The published online printing shop scheduling (OPS) problem is a real industrial
extension of the flexible job shop scheduling problem (FJSP). Here, "online"
refers to print orders placed through an online shop; it does not mean an online
algorithm that must commit to decisions as jobs arrive.

As in the flexible job shop problem in ../flexible_job_shop, every operation has
one or more eligible machine alternatives, and each alternative specifies a
machine and the processing duration on that machine.

The first defining extension is sequence flexibility. A job is not necessarily
a single fixed sequence of operations. Instead, its precedence constraints form
a directed acyclic graph (DAG). This allows independent branches to run in
parallel and later split or join. For example, a ganged printing operation may
be followed by cutting and then split into several independent product routes;
book covers and pages may also be produced in parallel before binding.

This initial example implements the smallest useful OPS core:

- choose exactly one eligible machine alternative for every operation;
- preserve every precedence edge in each job's DAG;
- prevent operations assigned to the same machine from overlapping;
- run each operation without interruption in this initial model; and
- minimize makespan, i.e. the completion time of the last operation.

Objective
---------

The objective is to minimize makespan:

    minimize  max over operations i of completion_i

This matches the objective studied by Lunardi et al. The published formulation
does not use due dates, priorities, or total weighted tardiness as its objective.
A due-date or weighted-tardiness FJSP variant may be useful as a separate
example, but it should not be presented as the OPS formulation from that paper.

Relation to the flexible job shop example
------------------------------------------

This is the smallest structural increment on top of ../flexible_job_shop:

- the machine-alternative structure is unchanged (routing flexibility);
- the linear operation sequence of each job is replaced by an arbitrary DAG
  (sequence flexibility); and
- the objective remains makespan minimization.

A classical FJSP job such as

    print -> cut -> bind

is therefore a special case. An OPS-style job may instead contain parallel,
splitting, and joining branches, for example:

    cover_print -----> cover_finish --+
                                      +--> bind
    page_print ------> page_fold -----+

Scope: grow one layer at a time
--------------------------------

The full OPS problem in the cited publication contains several additional
features. They are deliberately deferred from the first model so that each new
model adds one independently testable layer:

- release times for individual operations;
- sequence-dependent setup times;
- planned machine-unavailability periods;
- resumable processing across machine-unavailability periods (not arbitrary
  preemption: the operation remains assigned to the same machine and resumes as
  soon as that machine becomes available);
- fixed operations with preassigned machine and starting time; and
- partial overlap between operations connected by a precedence edge.

When release times are added, each operation must not start before its own
release time; this is a start-time lower bound to enforce, not just extra
data to carry alongside the operation.

When setup times and machine calendars are added, the full paper also requires
setups to be non-interruptible and to finish immediately before the associated
operation starts.

The publication explains ganging as an upstream cutting-stock decision that
merges printing operations from several customer orders. The resulting merged
structure is then given to the scheduling problem as a job; this initial example
does not optimize the cutting-stock or ganging decision itself.

Possible extensions such as due dates, weighted tardiness, operators, tools,
secondary resources, or alternative process plans may be valuable, but they are
beyond the formal OPS problem defined in the cited paper and should be labelled
separately rather than attributed to it.

Sourcing and provenance
------------------------

The online printing shop scheduling problem is a documented real industrial
problem, not an invented benchmark:

- Lunardi, Birgin, Laborie, Ronconi, and Voos, "Mixed Integer Linear
  Programming and Constraint Programming Models for the Online Printing Shop
  Scheduling Problem," Computers & Operations Research, 2020:
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2020.105020
  Preprint:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13064

- Authors' model and instance repository:
  https://github.com/Willtl/online-printing-shop

The repository homepage does not clearly state a license for the benchmark data.
Until a license is confirmed, do not copy its instances verbatim into this
directory. Any data file added under examples/online_printing_shop/ must either
be explicitly marked synthetic and kept small, or carry a confirmed license and
attribution, matching the provenance discipline already used for the
fjsp-instances data in ../flexible_job_shop (see
../../LICENSES/fjsp-instances.txt).

No model or data file exists yet in this directory; this problem.txt is the
first step.
